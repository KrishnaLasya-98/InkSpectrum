SOCIAL STUDIES · CLASS 5

Lesson 3

Worlds Weather and Climate

By the end of this chapter, learners will be able to:

Explain the difference between weather and climate.

Identify and describe the factors affecting climate.

Understand the different types of climate found in the world.

Recognize the climatic zones of the Earth.

Explain how climate affects human life and the environment.

Learning Objectives

Look outside the window! Is it sunny, cloudy, or rainy? Have you ever noticed how the weather changes throughout the day? Some places are always warm, while others are cold.

Why does this happen? In this chapter, we will explore the differences between weather and climate and understand the factors that influence them.

Start-Up

Weather is the condition of the atmosphere at a particular place and time. It includes temperature, wind, rainfall, humidity, and cloud cover.

Weather can change from hour to hour or day to day. For example, one morning, it may be sunny and by afternoon it could rain.

Weather

The hottest place on Earth is the Lut Desert in Iran, where temperatures have been recorded as high as 159.3°F (70.7°C)! Imagine how hot it must be to have that much heat!

Do You Know?

Climate is the average weather condition of a place over a long period, usually 30 years or more. It tells us whether a place is generally hot, cold, wet, or dry.

For example, the climate of the Sahara Desert is hot and dry, while the climate of the Amazon Rainforest is warm and rainy.

Climate

Several factors influence the climate of a place. Let us explore them one by one.

Factors Affecting Climate

The Earth is divided into imaginary lines called latitudes. Places near the equator receive direct sunlight throughout the year, making them hot.

As we move towards the poles, sunlight spreads out, making these areas cooler. This is why tropical regions are warm, while polar regions are cold.

Distance from the Equator (Latitudes)

Altitude is the height of a place above sea level. Higher altitudes have cooler temperatures because the air is thinner and cannot hold much heat. For example, mountains are colder than plains at the same latitude.

Altitude

Oceans and seas influence the climate of coastal areas. Water heats up and cools down more slowly than land.

During the day, land heats up faster than water, causing a sea breeze to blow from the sea to the land.

At night, the land cools down quickly, and a land breeze blows from land to the sea. This keeps coastal areas cooler in summer and warmer in winter compared to inland regions.

Distance from the Sea (Sea Breeze and Land Breeze)

Winds carry heat and moisture from one place to another. Winds coming from the sea bring rain, while winds from deserts bring dry weather. For example, the monsoon winds bring heavy rainfall to India.

Direction of the Wind

Humidity is the amount of water vapour in the air. Places with high humidity receive more rainfall, while dry areas have less humidity. For example, coastal areas have higher humidity than deserts.

Humidity

Ocean currents are large movements of water in the oceans. Warm ocean currents make coastal areas warmer, while cold currents bring cool temperatures.

For example, the Gulf Stream warms Europe, while the cold Labrador Current cools Canada.

Ocean Currents

Rainforests receive about 80 inches of rain each year and they are so important because they provide oxygen and food to the world. Without them, our planet would be very different!

Let's Learn More!

Different parts of the world have different types of climate. Let's look at some major types.

Types of Climate

Found in South Asia, Southeast Asia, and some parts of Africa.

It has hot summers and receives heavy rainfall during the monsoon season.

Monsoon or Tropical Climate

Found near the equator, such as in the Amazon Rainforest and Congo Basin.

It is hot and humid throughout the year with heavy rainfall.

Equatorial Climate

Found in places like the Sahara Desert and the Thar Desert.

It has very little rainfall, hot days, and cold nights.

Desert Climate

Found near the poles, such as in Greenland and Antarctica. It is extremely cold with little or no vegetation.

Tundra Climate

Antarctica is the driest, coldest, and windiest continent on Earth. It hasn’t rained there in millions of years, but it’s still considered a desert because of the lack of precipitation.

Do You Know?

The Earth is divided into three main climatic zones based on latitude.

Climatic Zones of the Earth

Located between the Tropic of Cancer and the Tropic of Capricorn.

It is the hottest zone as it receives direct sunlight.

Torrid Zone

Found between the tropics and the polar circles.

It has moderate temperatures with distinct seasons.

Temperate Zone

There are different types of clouds that can tell you about the weather? For example, cumulus clouds are fluffy and show fair weather, while nimbostratus clouds bring rain or snow.

Do you know?

Located near the poles.

It is the coldest zone with ice-covered lands.

Frigid Zone

Weather and climate are essential aspects of our environment. Many factors, such as latitude, altitude, wind, and ocean currents, influence climate.

Different regions experience different types of climates, affecting the way people live, the food they grow and the clothes they wear.

Recall

Weather is the daily atmospheric condition of a place.

Climate is the average weather over a long period.

Latitude, altitude, distance from the sea, and winds influence climate.

The world has different types of climate, including tropical, desert, and tundra.

The Earth has three climatic zones: torrid, temperate, and frigid.
