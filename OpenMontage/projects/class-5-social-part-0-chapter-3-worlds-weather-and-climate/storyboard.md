## s04-weather: Weather

Weather. Weather is the condition of the atmosphere at a particular place and time. It includes temperature, wind, rainfall, humidity, and cloud cover. Weather can change from hour to hour or day to day. For example, one morning, it may be sunny and by afternoon it could rain.

## s06-climate: Climate

Climate. Climate is the average weather condition of a place over a long period, usually 30 years or more. It tells us whether a place is generally hot, cold, wet, or dry. For example, the climate of the Sahara Desert is hot and dry, while the climate of the Amazon Rainforest is warm and rainy.

## s08-latitude: Distance from the Equator (Latitudes)

Distance from the Equator (Latitudes). The Earth is divided into imaginary lines called latitudes. Places near the equator receive direct sunlight throughout the year, making them hot. As we move towards the poles, sunlight spreads out, making these areas cooler. This is why tropical regions are warm, while polar regions are cold.

## s09-altitude: Altitude

Altitude. Altitude is the height of a place above sea level. Higher altitudes have cooler temperatures because the air is thinner and cannot hold much heat. For example, mountains are colder than plains at the same latitude.

Visual: An authored 3D terrain view moves from sea level to a snowy mountain; label altitude with precise editable text; original mountain photograph remains visible when referenced.

## s10-sea-land-breeze: Distance from the Sea (Sea Breeze and Land Breeze)

Distance from the Sea (Sea Breeze and Land Breeze). Oceans and seas influence the climate of coastal areas. Water heats up and cools down more slowly than land. During the day, land heats up faster than water, causing a sea breeze to blow from the sea to the land. At night, the land cools down quickly, and a land breeze blows from land to the sea. This keeps coastal areas cooler in summer and warmer in winter compared to inland regions.

Direction of the Wind. Winds carry heat and moisture from one place to another. Winds coming from the sea bring rain, while winds from deserts bring dry weather. For example, the monsoon winds bring heavy rainfall to India.

## s12-humidity: Humidity

Humidity. Humidity is the amount of water vapour in the air. Places with high humidity receive more rainfall, while dry areas have less humidity. For example, coastal areas have higher humidity than deserts.

## s13-currents: Ocean Currents

Ocean Currents. Ocean currents are large movements of water in the oceans. Warm ocean currents make coastal areas warmer, while cold currents bring cool temperatures. For example, the Gulf Stream warms Europe, while the cold Labrador Current cools Canada.
