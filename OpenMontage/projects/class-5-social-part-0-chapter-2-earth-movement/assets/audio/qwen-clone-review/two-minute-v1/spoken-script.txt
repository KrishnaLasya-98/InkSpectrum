Social Studies, Class 5. Lesson 2: Earth Movement. Learning Objectives. By the end of this chapter, learners will be able to: Understand that the Earth is spherical. Identify the two types of movements of the Earth. Explain rotation and its effects. Understand revolution and its effects. Learn about different seasons caused by revolution. Define solstices and equinoxes and when they occur. Start-Up Activity. Take a ball and spin it. Notice how it moves. Imagine this is the Earth moving in space. The Earth moves in two ways—let's explore these movements and their effects!

Who proved that the earth is spherical? Many ancient civilizations believed that the Earth was flat. However, Greek philosopher Pythagoras was one of the first to suggest that the Earth is spherical. Later, Aristotle provided evidence by observing how ships disappear over the horizon and how the Earth's shadow on the Moon is curved during a lunar eclipse. Ferdinand Magellan's voyage around the world further confirmed that the Earth is round. The Two Movements of the Earth. The Earth moves in two ways: Rotation – The Earth spins on its axis around itself. Revolution – The Earth moves around the Sun.

Rotation. Rotation is the movement of the Earth around its own axis is called Rotation. The earth rotates from west to east. It completes one full rotation in 24 hours. The speed of rotation is faster at the equator and slower at the poles. The axis is tilted at an angle of 23.5°. As the Earth is spherical in shape, only one half of the Earth receives sunlight at a time. Thus, the side of the Earth facing the Sun experience day. On the other hand, the other half, or the part away from the sun, experiences night. As the Earth rotates, the lighted part moves into the darkness. Rotation causes day and night. Do You Know? The Earth rotates at a speed of 1,670 kilometers per hour at the equator, but we don’t feel it because we’re moving along with it!
